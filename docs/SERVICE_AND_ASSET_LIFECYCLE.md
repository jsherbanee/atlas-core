## Scope
This lifecycle begins after delivery and continues through support, maintenance, upgrade, renewal, and retirement.

## Core Concepts
- installed assets
- serial numbers
- firmware
- configuration
- location
- warranty
- commissioning baseline
- service agreements
- preventive maintenance
- service tickets
- incidents
- troubleshooting history
- replacement parts
- manufacturer support
- lifecycle status
- end-of-life
- upgrade planning
- asset ownership transfer
- closeout handoff
- customer access
- recurring managed services

## Relationship To Earlier Records
Asset and service records should remain traceable back to:
- the original estimate
- procurement records where applicable
- installation records
- commissioning records
- closeout handoff records

## Lifecycle Status
Asset lifecycle status should communicate whether an asset is:
- active
- under warranty
- under service agreement
- at risk
- end-of-life
- replaced
- retired

## Differentiator
Atlas can become especially valuable when it connects project delivery to installed-system history and service operations over time.

## Operational Principles
- preserve source traceability
- keep installed history tenant-scoped
- support recurring service relationships
- keep warranty and support references explicit
- distinguish equipment history from project documents

## Future Direction
This lifecycle should eventually enable service continuity, replacement planning, and asset intelligence across the installed base.

## Unresolved Decisions
- final asset entity model remains open
- final service-agreement model remains open
- final customer-access model remains open
